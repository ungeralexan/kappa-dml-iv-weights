AE98 (ANGRIST & EVANS 1998) CHILDBEARING APPLICATION — CURRENT STATUS
=======================================================================

Last reconciled with the notebooks and saved exports: 22 July 2026


PURPOSE
-------
This application studies translation and scale behavior of 2SLS,
kappa-weighting, and DML estimators in the Angrist and Evans (1998)
childbearing design. The instrument is Z = samesex, the treatment is
D = morekids, and the outcomes are labor-force participation (workedm) and
log income (lincomem). The application mirrors the Card analysis while
respecting two AE98-specific features:

1. Labor and income require different analysis samples.
2. Smoother-matrix and outcome-weight diagnostics are run on two fixed,
   proportionally stratified 3,000-row diagnostic subsamples rather than on
   the 394,840-row labor and 220,502-row positive-income parent samples.

The design also contains an identification issue that must be kept distinct
from ordinary matrix collinearity. By construction,

    samesex = 1{sexk == sex2nd}.

Consequently, Z is deterministic conditional on a covariate vector containing
both sexk and sex2nd. Flexible nuisance learners can recover this relation, so
the true conditional instrument propensity is at the boundary. The notebooks
therefore compare a seven-covariate SUW-style working specification with a
documented six-covariate linear/logistic benchmark. The seven-covariate tree
learners are retained as specification stress tests; they do not establish
conditional overlap.


FILES AND HOW THEY FIT TOGETHER
-------------------------------

- ae98_make_balanced_subsample_submission.Rmd
  Builds and saves the two fixed 3,000-row diagnostic samples in ae98_sub.rds:

    ae98_labor_sub
      Stratified proportionally on samesex, morekids, workedm, incomepos,
      race, and age_bin.

    ae98_income_sub
      Drawn from the positive-income parent population and stratified
      proportionally on samesex, morekids, income decile, race, and age_bin.

  The RDS also contains an obsolete ae98_sub alias for ae98_labor_sub. Do not
  use that alias.

- ae98_descriptives.Rmd
  Documents the two samples, instrument/treatment shares, conventional IV
  building blocks, covariate balance, the additive-logit propensity diagnostic,
  and the deterministic relation between sexk, sex2nd, and samesex. The
  additive-logit propensity plot is a working-model diagnostic only. Because
  the additive logit omits the equality/XOR relation, probabilities near 0.5
  must not be described as proof of full-X conditional overlap.

- child_test.Rmd
  Main analysis notebook:

    Section 1: SUW Table 4 structure on the diagnostic samples. Conventional
      2SLS and six kappa estimators use all seven controls: agem, agefstm,
      sexk, sex2nd, blackm, hispm, and othracem.

    Section 2: dml_with_smoother() with the full seven-covariate design.
      PLR-IV and Wald-AIPW are estimated with untuned and tuned honest forests,
      five-fold cross-fitting, extracted outcome weights, and identity checks.

    Section 3: learner comparison. Linear/logistic PLR-IV and Wald-AIPW use
      the six-covariate design that drops sex2nd. Ranger and XGBoost use all
      seven covariates. Ranger is fitted in the notebook; fitted XGBoost
      estimates are imported from the separate tuning exports.

    Sections 4–5: exact weight diagnostics and Love plots. XGBoost point
      estimates remain visible as learner-sensitivity results, but XGBoost
      weights enter exact diagnostics only when sum(omega_i Y_i) reproduces
      the reported coefficient. All current child XGBoost identity flags are
      FALSE, so those weights are excluded from the exact diagnostic blocks.

- child_xgb_tuning.Rmd
  Fits DoubleMLIIVM/Wald-AIPW with XGBoost for workedm and lincomem, using the
  full seven-covariate design. It compares untuned and tuned fits. Tuning is
  nested inside the five outer cross-fitting folds: three-fold inner CV,
  15 random-search evaluations, and nuisance-prediction loss only. The fitted
  results, fold-specific tuning information, nuisance losses, outcome weights,
  identity flags, settings, and sample metadata are saved to
  ae98_xgb_tuning_export.rds. child_test.Rmd imports these fitted outputs and
  does not create a new fit from median hyperparameters.

- child_xgb_pliv_tuning.Rmd
  Parallel DoubleMLPLIV/XGBoost analysis with the same seven covariates,
  five outer folds, tune_on_folds = TRUE, three inner folds, and 15 random
  evaluations. The current notebook has EXTRACT_WEIGHTS = TRUE. Estimates,
  nuisance losses, fold-specific tuning information, extracted weights,
  identity flags, settings, and sample metadata are saved to
  ae98_xgb_pliv_tuning_export.rds.

- child_translation_invariance_method_a.Rmd
  Complete-pipeline rerun test on fixed rows. It compares workedm with
  workedm + 1 and lincomem with lincomem + log(100). It covers normalized and
  unnormalized kappa estimators, tuned honest forests, the six-covariate
  linear/logistic benchmark, seven-covariate Ranger, and tuned XGBoost.
  workedm_01 = 1 - workedm is excluded because it is a reflection, not a

- pliv_xgboost_weight_note.txt
  Records the current PLR-IV and Wald-AIPW XGBoost weight status. Both sets of

1. ESTIMATOR-SPECIFICATION MAP
------------------------------

Full seven-covariate design
  - Conventional 2SLS
  - CBPS and MLE kappa estimators
  - dml_with_smoother() honest forest
  - Ranger PLR-IV and Wald-AIPW
  - XGBoost PLR-IV and Wald-AIPW

Reduced six-covariate design (sex2nd omitted)
  - Linear PLR-IV
  - Linear/logistic Wald-AIPW

The six-covariate design was originally introduced because arm-specific
parametric nuisance regressions are rank deficient with both sex indicators.
It also has a substantive interpretation: after omitting one component of Z,
samesex is no longer mechanically known from the conditioning vector. This is
the only current child specification that directly avoids deterministic Z|X.

The specifications must therefore be described as follows:

  - Seven-covariate 2SLS/kappa: SUW-style parametric working specification.
  - Six-covariate linear/logit DML: reduced-conditioning-set benchmark.
  - Seven-covariate flexible learners: stress tests showing what happens when
    the learner can recover the instrument's deterministic construction.

Do not describe all rows as estimates under one common conditioning set.


2. CONDITIONAL-OVERLAP FINDING
------------------------------

The additive pooled logit used by the kappa estimators produces fitted
instrument propensities near one half because it includes sexk and sex2nd
additively and omits their equality/XOR relation. This is computationally
well behaved but does not establish the Abadie overlap condition for the true
P(Z=1|X) when X contains both sex indicators.

The fitted XGBoost IIVM models recover the deterministic construction almost
perfectly. The share of instrument-nuisance predictions below 0.02 or above
0.98 is:

                          Untuned       Tuned
  Labor / workedm          99.30%       99.83%
  Income / lincomem        98.67%       99.70%

Instrument log-loss is approximately 0.002. The currently printed combined
"propensity boundary share" pools the instrument predictions m(X) with the
treatment predictions r(Z,X). Its values near 0.50 therefore mask the fact
that essentially all m(X) predictions are at the boundary. Any future table
should report instrument and treatment boundary shares separately.

Ranger smooths more strongly: its fitted instrument probabilities remain
roughly between 0.10 and 0.91. That is regularization behavior, not evidence
that population overlap has been restored.

Empirical implication:
  - Wald-AIPW estimates from six-covariate linear/logit, seven-covariate
    Ranger, and seven-covariate honest forest are fairly similar.
  - Flexible PLR-IV, especially XGBoost, is extremely unstable because a
    flexible learner removes almost all residual instrument variation.
  - XGBoost should remain an estimate-level stress test, not a headline
    causal estimate or exact weight benchmark.

4. REMAINING DECISIONS AND TASKS
--------------------------------

Required before final thesis interpretation
  1. Choose the headline interpretation. The recommended structure is:
       - seven-covariate 2SLS/kappa as SUW parametric replication;
       - six-covariate linear/logit as the reduced-X benchmark;
       - seven-covariate flexible learners as overlap/specification stress
         tests.
  2. Rewrite the descriptive overlap language. The additive-logit plot may
     describe the fitted working model, but it must not claim that full-X
     conditional overlap holds.
  3. Report m(X) and r(Z,X) boundary diagnostics separately if the XGBoost
     diagnostics remain in the appendix.
  4. Label the 3,000-row estimates as diagnostic-subsample results rather
     than a full-sample AE98 replication. Ordinary SEs do not incorporate a
     separate finite-population stratified-sampling correction.

Useful robustness checks
  - Refit kappa and Ranger on the six-covariate design to quantify how much
    changing the conditioning set moves the estimates.
  - Consider repeated cross-fitting for the stochastic learners. Current
    n_reps is one.
  - Add strict validation of imported XGBoost exports against current data,
    ideally with a row/data hash rather than sample moments alone.

Not currently necessary
  - Raising XGBoost TUNE_EVALS merely to seek a more favorable treatment-
    effect estimate. The present instability is substantively informative
    and cannot be tuned away without changing the design question.


5. HARD RULES
-------------

1. Use ae98_labor_sub and ae98_income_sub explicitly; never use the obsolete
   ae98_sub alias and never mix the two outcome samples.
2. Do not describe the diagnostic-subsample tables as reproducing the full
   published observation counts.
3. Do not silently remove sex2nd. Any six-covariate result must be labeled as
   a reduced-conditioning-set specification and separated from the exact
   seven-covariate SUW-style specification.
4. Do not claim that pooled-logit propensities near 0.5 prove overlap. They
   reflect an additive working model that cannot represent the deterministic
   equality relation defining samesex.
5. Do not describe tree learners as "unaffected" by the sex-variable issue.
   They avoid matrix inversion failure, but flexible learners can discover
   deterministic Z|X, which is an identification/overlap problem.
6. Tune nuisance learners only on nuisance-prediction loss, never on the
   treatment-effect estimate.
7. Do not plug fold-median XGBoost hyperparameters into a new main-notebook
   fit. tune_on_folds = TRUE creates fold-specific choices; import the fitted
   export instead.
8. Reseed immediately before stochastic fits that must be reproducible or
   compared with a counterpart run.
9. The SUW additive-inverse sign change for the "did not work" reproduction
   column is display-only. Do not apply it elsewhere.
10. Use only pure outcome translations in translation-invariance tests and
    hold the exact sample rows fixed.
11. Do not use XGBoost outcome weights in exact ESS, balance, or Love-plot
    claims while their identity flags are FALSE. The point estimates may be
    shown transparently as learner-sensitivity results.
